# Slide Layout Contracts

Select layouts by communication job. Preserve the supplied template's master, margins, type, and footer system.

## Cover

- Use one title, one concise subtitle or context line, and only required brand or confidentiality marks.
- Prefer a real product hero image or approved brand visual when available.
- Do not use feature cards, status pills, or a dashboard-style grid on the cover.

## Product screen explanation

- Make the actual product screenshot the dominant visual.
- Add numbered callouts directly around or over the screenshot without hiding important controls.
- Pair callout numbers with a compact explanation table or aligned text list.
- Keep the mapping one-to-one and preserve the screenshot's aspect ratio.
- If the screenshot is unreadable at slide scale, crop into a focused real-screen detail instead of redrawing it.

## Procedure

- Show a clear ordered path with three to seven steps.
- Use a horizontal sequence for short linear flows and a vertical sequence for text-heavy steps.
- Include real screen crops only where a visual decision is necessary.
- Use one consistent step-number treatment; do not wrap every step in a separate decorative card.

## Comparison

- Use aligned columns, a matrix, or a before/after split with shared criteria.
- Keep labels and baselines directly comparable.
- Highlight only the decisive difference with the deck's single accent color.
- Do not create a row of repeated marketing cards.

## Checklist

- Use a simple checklist, verification matrix, or grouped readiness list.
- Reserve status pills for real state values such as Ready, Blocked, or Required.
- Keep ownership, evidence, and status in aligned columns when they are part of the decision.

## Section or transition

- Use a short section title and one orienting statement.
- Create pacing with whitespace or one approved visual, not multiple cards.

## Executive summary

- Lead with the decision, outcome, or critical metric.
- Support it with no more than two evidence groups.
- Separate facts, risks, and next actions without turning each sentence into a card.
