# Document Design Quality Checklist

Use this checklist for reports, manuals, Word/PDF documents, spreadsheets, templates, and print-oriented deliverables.

## Structure and reading path

- Give the document a clear purpose, audience, and reading sequence.
- Use a restrained hierarchy for title, section, subsection, body, caption, note, and metadata.
- Keep navigation aids such as contents, section markers, page numbers, and running headers consistent.
- Avoid repeating an identical card or panel composition on every page.

## Typography and page system

- Use readable line lengths, line height, paragraph spacing, and margins at the target page size.
- Keep font families and weights limited and purposeful.
- Prevent orphaned headings, isolated captions, accidental blank pages, and awkward page breaks.
- Apply one coherent grid and align text, images, tables, and callouts to it.

## Evidence, tables, and graphics

- Preserve supplied facts, citations, screenshots, and brand assets.
- Keep captions and source notes adjacent to the material they explain.
- Favor direct labels and comparable baselines in tables and charts.
- Do not fabricate metrics, product screens, logos, or decorative evidence.
- Preserve image aspect ratios and ensure screenshots remain readable.

## Visual restraint and brand

- Reuse supplied templates, colors, typography, logo rules, and confidentiality marks.
- Use accent colors sparingly and consistently.
- Use boxes, cards, pills, icons, and illustrations only when they clarify structure or meaning.
- Reject generic AI-template patterns that overpower the organization, subject, or evidence.

## Exported-output review

- Render or export the final document in its target format.
- Inspect every page for clipping, overlap, overflow, broken tables, missing fonts, low contrast, and distorted media.
- Check page sequence, headers, footers, numbering, links, bookmarks, and print margins when applicable.
- Review representative pages at normal reading size and the complete thumbnail sequence for rhythm and repetition.
